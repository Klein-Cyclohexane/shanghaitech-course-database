\item \defpoints{15} [Convolution and Cross-Correlation]

(a) Compute cross-correlation and convolution of $A$ and $B$ (Use $B$ as the filter), respectively. Both the operations are with zero-padding, $P=1$, and stride is set to be 1. \defpoints{12}

$$
A=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}
2 & 4 & -3 \\
3 & -2 & 2 \\
-3 & 1 & 4
\end{array}\right], \quad B=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}
3 & 1 & -2 \\
-2 & 2 & 4 \\
1 & 3 & -3
\end{array}\right]
$$

(b) Under what circumstances does convolution have the same result as correlation? \defpoints{3}

[Hint: Please notice the mathmatical definition of convolution and cross-correlation.]

\solution

(a)\\
With padding P=1, the matrices become:
$$
A_{padded}=\left[\begin{array}{ccccc}
0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 2 & 4 & -3 & 0 \\
0 & 3 & -2 & 2 & 0 \\
0 & -3 & 1 & 4 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0
\end{array}\right]
$$

For cross-correlation, we slide filter $B$ over $A_{padded}$ without flipping.\\

$C_{corr}(0,0) = 0\cdot3 + 0\cdot1 + 0\cdot(-2) + 0\cdot(-2) + 2\cdot2 + 4\cdot4 + 0\cdot1 + 3\cdot3 + (-2)\cdot(-3) = 4 + 16 + 9 + 6 = 35$\\

Computing all positions systematically:

$$
\text{Cross-correlation} = \left[\begin{array}{ccc}
35 & -17 & -10 \\
-20 & 2 & 30 \\
5 & 27 & 2
\end{array}\right]
$$

For convolution, we first flip filter $B$ horizontally and vertically:\\
$$
B_{flipped}=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}
-3 & 3 & 1 \\
4 & 2 & -2 \\
-2 & 1 & 3
\end{array}\right]
$$

Then we perform cross-correlation with this flipped filter:\\

Output at position (0,0): $0\cdot(-3) + 0\cdot3 + 0\cdot1 + 0\cdot4 + 2\cdot2 + 4\cdot(-2) + 0\cdot(-2) + 3\cdot1 + (-2)\cdot3 = 4 - 8 + 3 - 6 = -7$\\

Computing all positions systematically:

$$
\text{Convolution} = \left[\begin{array}{ccc}
-7 & 20 & 16 \\
20 & 26 & -23 \\
-1 & -31 & 24
\end{array}\right]
$$

(b) Convolution has the same result as cross-correlation when the filter is symmetric about both its horizontal and vertical axes (i.e., when the filter is centro-symmetric). That means the filter is invariant under 180-degree rotation. In other words, the filter looks the same when flipped in both dimensions.\\

Mathematically, this means the filter $B$ satisfies $B[i,j] = B[-i,-j]$ for all valid indices. This is because flipping the filter in both directions would yield the same filter, making convolution equivalent to cross-correlation.\\

Examples of such filters include:\\
- Single value filters\\
- Filters with rotational symmetry of order 2 (180° rotational symmetry)\\
- Certain filters like $\begin{bmatrix}a & b & a\\c & d & c\\a & b & a\end{bmatrix}$ that have reflection symmetry across both axes
